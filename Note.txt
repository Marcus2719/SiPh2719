## 2) 12‑Week Detailed Agenda (2 sessions/week, 24 sessions total)

### Week 1 — Foundations: Platforms, Modes, and Confinement
**Session 1: Silicon photonics landscape + waveguide physics basics**
- Why silicon photonics: CMOS compatibility, density, cost, limitations (TPA, free‑carrier absorption).
- Index contrast and confinement: Si (n~3.48) vs SiO₂ (n~1.44), SiN (n~2.0).
- Guided modes, effective index, TE/TM in high‑contrast waveguides.
- Mode solvers overview: eigenmode (FDE/FEM), EME, FDTD (when/why).
- Deliverable: define **TE0** and **TM0**; explain effective index and confinement factor qualitatively.

**Session 2: Platform comparison — Si vs SOI vs SiN**
- **Si bulk vs SOI**: why SOI dominates (buried oxide isolation, confinement, thermal).
- **SiN platform**: ultra‑low loss, large bends, weaker modulation (often requires heterogeneous or thermal tuning).
- **Waveguide cross‑sections**: strip vs rib; shallow etch vs full etch.
- Trade table: propagation loss, bend radius, nonlinearities, thermal stability, manufacturing tolerance.
- Mini‑exercise: choose platform for (a) coherent transceiver, (b) biosensing, (c) nonlinear comb source.

---

### Week 2 — Waveguide Categories + Loss Mechanisms (Deep Dive)
**Session 3: Waveguide categories (required topic)**
Cover the main categories and why they exist:
- **Strip waveguides**, **rib waveguides**
- **Slot waveguides** (field in low‑index slot; sensing, EO polymers)
- **Subwavelength grating (SWG) waveguides** (engineered effective index)
- **Photonic crystal waveguides** (slow light; fabrication sensitivity)
- **Hybrid plasmonic waveguides** (confinement vs loss trade‑off)
- Polarization handling: polarization rotators/splitters (high‑level).
- Deliverable: a one‑page map: category → use case → key trade‑offs.

**Session 4: Waveguide loss: physics and design strategies**
- Loss sources:
  - **Sidewall roughness scattering**
  - **material absorption** (Si, SiN, cladding)
  - **bend radiation loss**
  - **substrate leakage** (esp. thin BOX)
  - **free carrier absorption (FCA)** (doped waveguides)
- Wavelength dependence and polarization dependence of loss.
- Manufacturing impacts: etch bias, line edge roughness, thickness variation.
- Practical numbers: Si strip ~0.5–3 dB/cm typical (foundry‑dependent); SiN can reach <<0.1 dB/cm in specialized processes.
- Mini‑exercise: identify which loss dominates in (i) tight bend (ii) heavily doped phase shifter (iii) long SiN delay line.

---

### Week 3 — Waveguide Loss Measurement + Metrology
**Session 5: Propagation loss measurement (required topic)**
- **Cut‑back method**: principle, fabrication needs (multiple lengths), error sources.
- **Fabry–Pérot method**: using facet reflections; extracting loss and group index.
- **Ring resonator method**: intrinsic Q, loaded Q; converting Q to propagation loss.  
  - Relationship: \(Q_i \leftrightarrow \alpha\) using group index and round‑trip loss (derive in session).
- What to report: dB/cm, uncertainty, polarization, wavelength, temperature.
- Deliverable: draft a measurement plan for extracting loss with ring resonators + cutback cross‑check.

**Session 6: Characterization toolkit**
- Optical setup blocks: tunable laser, polarization controller, fiber array, photodetector, power meter, OSA.
- Calibration: reference waveguides, grating coupler normalization.
- Extracting **group index** from MZI fringes or ring FSR:  
  \(FSR \approx \lambda^2/(n_g L)\).
- Temperature dependence: thermo‑optic coefficient (Si large), thermal drift.
- Mini‑exercise: compute \(n_g\) from given FSR and ring circumference.

---

### Week 4 — Fiber‑Chip Coupling + Grating Couplers (Major Required)
**Session 7: Fiber‑chip coupling overview (required topic)**
- Coupling methods:
  - **Edge coupling (butt coupling)** to inverse tapers / spot‑size converters
  - **Grating coupling** (surface normal or angled fiber)
- Trade‑offs: bandwidth, alignment tolerance, polarization sensitivity, back reflection.
- Packaging constraints: fiber array pitch, epoxy, thermal expansion, reliability.
- Deliverable: compare edge vs grating coupling for high‑volume transceiver packaging.

**Session 8: Grating couplers in detail (required topic)**
- Phase matching: grating period \(\Lambda\), effective index, emission angle.
- **Apodized gratings**, **focusing gratings**, **bottom reflectors** (to improve directionality).
- **1D vs 2D gratings** (polarization handling).
- Bandwidth limits and wavelength dependence; typical coupling loss.
- Back reflection and its impact on lasers.
- Mini‑exercise: given \(n_\text{eff}\), target wavelength, and fiber angle, estimate \(\Lambda\) qualitatively.

---

### Week 5 — Waveguide Bends: L, U, S Bends (Required)
**Session 9: Bend physics + design rules**
- **Adiabatic vs non‑adiabatic bending**
- Bend loss dependence on radius, mode confinement, polarization.
- Euler bends / clothoids for lower transition loss.
- Foundry bend libraries and why.
- Deliverable: propose bend radius guidelines for Si strip vs SiN.

**Session 10: Specific bend types (required: waveguide bends, S, L, U)**
- **L‑bends (90°)**: corner rounding, multi‑mode risk, radiation.
- **U‑bends (180°)**: compactness vs loss/crosstalk.
- **S‑bends**: offset routing; adiabatic S‑bends vs compact cosine bends.
- Coupling/crosstalk between adjacent bends; minimum spacing rules.
- Mini‑exercise: choose S‑bend length to limit mode conversion (qualitative + discuss curvature constraints).

---

### Week 6 — Power Splitters/Combiners and Interference Building Blocks
**Session 11: Y‑branch (required)**
- Y‑branch principle: adiabatic splitting, fabrication sensitivity at the tip.
- Excess loss, imbalance, broadband nature.
- Compare to MMI splitter: footprint vs tolerance.
- Deliverable: design decision: Y‑branch vs MMI for broadband 1×2 splitter.

**Session 12: MMI (required) + interference fundamentals**
- Self‑imaging in multimode waveguides; why MMIs are robust.
- 1×2, 2×2 MMI; phase relations and imbalance.
- Sensitivity to width/length errors.
- Mini‑exercise: interpret an MMI transfer matrix qualitatively; discuss phase errors in MZI.

---

### Week 7 — Directional Couplers + MZI/MZM (Required)
**Session 13: Directional coupler (required)**
- Coupled‑mode theory: even/odd supermodes, coupling length.
- Wavelength sensitivity; polarization dependence.
- Gap sensitivity and manufacturing variability.
- Adiabatic couplers (broadband) vs straight directional couplers.
- Deliverable: compare DC vs MMI as 2×2 coupler in MZI.

**Session 14: Mach–Zehnder interferometer and MZM (required)**
- MZI transfer function; bias point; extinction ratio.
- **MZM** as modulator: push‑pull drive benefits, chirp control.
- Insertion loss and imbalance (coupler errors, phase shifter loss).
- Metrics: \(V_\pi L\), ER, IL, bandwidth, energy/bit.
- Mini‑exercise: compute required phase shift for ER target; discuss bias control.

---

### Week 8 — Microrings, Filters, and AWG (Required)
**Session 15: Microring resonator (MRM) fundamentals (required)**
- Resonance condition; FSR; loaded vs intrinsic Q; critical coupling.
- Ring as filter, modulator, sensor.
- Thermal tuning and stabilization.
- Nonlinear effects in rings (Kerr, TPA) preview.
- Deliverable: choose ring radius for target channel spacing (e.g., 100 GHz) conceptually.

**Session 16: AWG (required) + WDM interconnect architecture**
- AWG principle: phased array interference; free‑propagation region (FPR).
- Channel spacing, insertion loss, crosstalk, phase errors.
- Compare AWG vs rings for WDM: thermal sensitivity, footprint, passband shape.
- Mini‑exercise: draft a WDM demux choice for 8 channels: AWG vs ring bank with pros/cons.

---

### Week 9 — Electro‑Optic Effects in Silicon: Plasma Dispersion + Nonlinearities
**Session 17: Plasma dispersion effect (required)**
- Carrier‑induced refractive index change and absorption change in Si.
- Link to Soref–Bennett model (conceptual + cite).
- Trade‑off: phase modulation vs absorption (loss increases with carriers).
- PN junction layouts in waveguides: lateral, vertical, interdigitated.
- Deliverable: explain why stronger modulation typically increases loss in Si.

**Session 18: Kerr effect + two‑photon absorption (required)**
- **Kerr (χ(3))**: intensity‑dependent index; self‑phase modulation.
- **Two‑photon absorption (TPA)** in Si at 1550 nm → generates free carriers → FCA.
- Effective nonlinear parameter \(\gamma\) and dependence on effective area.
- Mitigation strategies: use SiN, longer wavelengths (mid‑IR), reverse bias sweep‑out, p‑i‑n engineering.
- Mini‑exercise: discuss why SiN is preferred for low‑noise nonlinear optics (frequency combs).

---

### Week 10 — Modulators: PN Junction Modes + Electro‑Absorption
**Session 19: PN junction modulator modes (required)**
Cover all three explicitly:
- **Carrier depletion** (reverse bias): fast, lower loss, moderate efficiency.
- **Carrier injection** (forward bias): higher efficiency, slower (carrier lifetime), more loss.
- **Carrier accumulation** (MOS capacitor): potentially efficient and fast; process complexity.
- Device metrics: \(V_\pi L\), RC limits, optical loss, linearity, chirp.
- Deliverable: fill a comparison table (depletion vs injection vs accumulation) for 25–100 Gb/s links.

**Session 20: Electro‑absorption modulator (EAM) (required)**
- Why Si itself is weak for linear Pockels (χ(2) absent in bulk Si); EAM often via:
  - **Germanium/SiGe Franz–Keldysh** effect
  - **III–V/Si heterogeneous** (QCSE)
- Compare EAM vs MZM vs ring modulator: footprint, drive voltage, chirp, temperature.
- Practical integration: insertion loss and photothermal effects.
- Mini‑exercise: select modulator architecture for short‑reach datacom vs long‑reach coherent.

---

### Week 11 — Photodetectors: GeSi, MQW, APD (Required)
**Session 21: GeSi photodetector (required)**
- Germanium on silicon: bandgap enables 1310/1550 nm absorption.
- PIN Ge PD: responsivity, dark current, bandwidth (transit time + RC).
- Integration challenges: defects, thermal budget, coupling (evanescent vs butt).
- Deliverable: make a PD metric sheet: responsivity, bandwidth, dark current, saturation power.

**Session 22: MQW PD + Ge APD (required)**
- **MQW PD** (often III–V): quantum well absorption; high speed, high responsivity; integration routes (bonding).
- **Ge APD**: avalanche gain, excess noise, breakdown voltage, bandwidth–gain trade‑off.
- Receiver sensitivity concepts: noise sources (shot, thermal, RIN), TIA impact.
- Mini‑exercise: discuss when APD is worth it vs PIN (power budget vs complexity).

---

### Week 12 — RF Co‑Design + System Integration + Capstone
**Session 23: RF signal design — Lumped vs Traveling‑Wave (required)**
- Lumped electrodes: short devices, RC limitation; low complexity.
- Traveling‑wave electrodes (TWE): impedance matching (≈50 Ω), microwave index matching to optical group velocity, termination.
- Bandwidth limits: conductor loss, dielectric loss, velocity mismatch.
- Electro‑optic S‑parameters: \(S_{11}\), \(S_{21}\), eye diagrams (conceptual).
- Deliverable: choose lumped vs TWE for a 2–5 mm phase shifter at 50–70 GHz target.

**Session 24: Capstone integration + full link budget**
- Build a **complete silicon photonics link** on paper:
  - Laser input → coupler (edge or grating) → splitter/coupler → modulator (MZM or ring) → WDM (AWG or rings) → waveguide routing (bends/S‑bends) → detector (Ge PIN or APD)
- Budgets:
  - **Optical loss budget** (dB): coupling + propagation + component IL
  - **Extinction ratio**, **OSNR** (qualitative), **receiver sensitivity**
  - **Electrical energy/bit**, driver constraints
  - Thermal tuning power and stabilization plan
- Final group output: 6–10 slide presentation + a 1–2 page “design justification” with assumptions.

## 3) Extra “Related Contents” (Integrated Across Weeks or as Optional Reading)

To enrich the plan beyond the required list, the study group should also touch:
1. **Thermo‑optic tuning & heaters** (power consumption, time constants, drift).  
2. **Phase noise / laser linewidth implications** (especially for coherent or resonant devices).  
3. **Back reflection and isolator absence in silicon PICs** (how couplers and gratings matter).  
4. **Polarization management** (PSR/PR, polarization diversity).  
5. **Process design kit (PDK) literacy**: design rules, density fill, metal routing constraints.  
6. **Testing structures**: alignment marks, cutback structures, ring sweeps, MZI arrays.  
7. **Yield and variability**: Monte Carlo with width/thickness variations; trimming/calibration strategies.  
8. **Nonlinear impairments in high power**: TPA/FCA in Si; thermal bistability in rings.  
9. **Packaging**: fiber attach, RF probes vs wirebond vs flip‑chip, co‑packaged optics concepts.
